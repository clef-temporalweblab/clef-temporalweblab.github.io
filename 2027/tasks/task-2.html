Task 2 · Retrieval and grounded QA

Question Answering over Web Archives

Answer questions about past information and changes over time using evidence from archived Web pages.

Overview

Web pages are updated, replaced, or removed, making it difficult to recover earlier information from the current Web. Web archives preserve captures of pages from different times, allowing systems to examine past information and compare versions.

Task 2 asks participants to retrieve relevant evidence from a fixed archive collection and answer questions about information at specific times or changes across archived versions. Each answer must include the identifiers of supporting archive records.

Provisional specification: Corpus size, redistribution mechanism, final schemas, and scoring details remain subject to organizer validation.

Input, output and example

Input

A temporal question and access to the frozen End of Term–based corpus released for the campaign.

Output

An answer and the stable record identifiers of supporting archive evidence.

Illustrative example

This synthetic example illustrates the task; the program, values, and record IDs below are fictional.

Archived evidence Record example-2016: a 2016 capture of the Example Research Grant page states a maximum award of $10,000.
Record example-2020: a 2020 capture of the same page states a maximum award of $15,000.
Question How did the maximum award listed for the Example Research Grant change between the 2016 and 2020 captures?
Answer It increased from $10,000 to $15,000.
Supporting record IDs example-2016, example-2020

Question types

Point in time

Identify information recorded in archive captures relevant to a specified date or period.

First or last observed

Identify the earliest or latest observation of a fact within the released archive collection. The first recorded observation does not necessarily indicate when the fact became true.

Compare versions

Compare values or statements across two archived states.

Verify change

Determine whether the specified change is supported by the archived versions.

Trace changes

Reconstruct a sequence of observed changes across archived versions.

Corpus and dataset splits

The pilot corpus is planned around selected End of Term collections from 2012, 2016, 2020, and 2024. Each processed record will retain stable identifiers and temporal metadata so systems can cite evidence reproducibly.

Planned record contents

  • Stable record identifier and source URL.
  • Capture timestamp and collection provenance.
  • Extracted text suitable for retrieval.
  • Available document-level metadata.
Component Planned scale
Frozen corpus Approximately 1–3 million WET-derived records (about 15–40 GB).
Training questions Approximately 2,000 with answers and evidence.
Development questions Approximately 200 with answers and evidence.
Test questions Approximately 200 with hidden answers and evidence.

Participants may build lexical, dense, or hybrid indexes. A BM25 index and starter retrieval results are planned.

Evaluation

Component Planned measures
Answer quality Normalized exact match and token-level F1.
Typed answers Type-specific comparison for dates, intervals, numbers, and sets.
Multiple changes Set-based precision, recall, and F1.
Evidence grounding Record-level evidence precision, recall, and F1.

The official ranking formula and treatment of answer/evidence components will be finalized before the evaluation period.

Baselines

Planned baselines include BM25 retrieval, standard retrieve-then-answer pipelines, and evidence-aware answer generation. Baseline outputs will preserve official record IDs.

Submission and participation

Each prediction will contain the question ID, answer, and supporting record IDs. Teams may submit up to three runs, subject to confirmation in the final rules.

The released corpus defines the official evidence universe. Rules for live-Web access, external models, and auxiliary resources will be stated before submissions open.

Task 2 FAQ

Must teams use the planned BM25 index?

No. Teams may build dense, sparse, or hybrid indexes over the frozen corpus while preserving official record IDs.

Why are record IDs required?

They make evidence evaluation reproducible and separate grounded answers from unsupported answers.

Can systems search the live Web?

The final system-boundary rules will state whether any external access is permitted. Official evidence must come from the frozen corpus.
