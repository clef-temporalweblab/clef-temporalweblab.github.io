Task 2 · Retrieval and grounded QA

Question Answering over Web Archives

Retrieve evidence from a frozen archive corpus and answer questions about what the Web supported at particular times and how that information changed.

Overview

Web pages evolve, disappear, and contradict earlier versions. Task 2 evaluates systems that reconstruct temporally grounded answers from archived evidence instead of relying on the current Web or parametric memory alone.

Provisional specification: Corpus size, redistribution mechanism, final schemas, and scoring details remain subject to organizer validation.

Input, output and example

Input

A temporal question and access to the frozen End of Term–based corpus released for the campaign.

Output

A normalized answer together with the stable record identifiers of supporting archive evidence.

Illustrative example

QuestionWhat value was reported on the agency page in 2016?
AnswerThe normalized value found in the archived evidence.
EvidenceOne or more stable record IDs from the 2016 corpus.

Question types

Point in time

Determine what information was supported at a specified date or period.

First or last observed

Identify the earliest or latest archived observation of a fact.

Compare versions

Compare values or statements across two archived states.

Verify or trace change

Confirm whether a change occurred or reconstruct multiple changes over time.

Corpus and dataset splits

The pilot corpus is planned around selected End of Term collections from 2012, 2016, 2020, and 2024. Each processed record will retain stable identifiers and temporal metadata so systems can cite evidence reproducibly.

Planned record contents

  • Stable record identifier and source URL.
  • Capture timestamp and collection provenance.
  • Extracted text suitable for retrieval.
  • Available document-level metadata.
ComponentPlanned scale
Frozen corpusApproximately 1–3 million WET-derived records (about 15–40 GB).
Training questionsApproximately 2,000 with answers and evidence.
Development questionsApproximately 200 with answers and evidence.
Test questionsApproximately 200 with hidden answers and evidence.

Participants may build lexical, dense, or hybrid indexes. A BM25 index and starter retrieval results are planned.

Evaluation

ComponentPlanned measures
Answer qualityNormalized exact match and token-level F1.
Typed answersType-specific comparison for dates, intervals, numbers, and sets.
Multiple changesSet-based precision, recall, and F1.
Evidence groundingRecord-level evidence precision, recall, and F1.

The official ranking formula and treatment of answer/evidence components will be finalized before the evaluation period.

Baselines

Planned baselines include BM25 retrieval, standard retrieve-then-answer pipelines, and evidence-aware answer generation. Baseline outputs will preserve official record IDs.

Submission and participation

Each prediction will contain the question ID, answer, and supporting record IDs. Teams may submit up to three runs, subject to confirmation in the final rules.

The released corpus defines the official evidence universe. Rules for live-Web access, external models, and auxiliary resources will be stated before submissions open.

Task 2 FAQ

Must teams use the supplied BM25 index?

No. Teams may build dense, sparse, or hybrid indexes over the frozen corpus while preserving official record IDs.

Why are record IDs required?

They make evidence evaluation reproducible and separate grounded answers from unsupported answers.

Can systems search the live Web?

The final system-boundary rules will state whether any external access is permitted. Official evidence must come from the frozen corpus.
